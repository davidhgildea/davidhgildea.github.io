1 Corinthians 8:1 About food given to idols, we understand that we all have knowledge. Knowledge makes one proud, but love builds up.
1 Corinthians 8:2 If someone thinks they know something, they don't know it as well as they should.
1 Corinthians 8:3 If anyone loves God, that person is known by God.
1 Corinthians 8:4 About eating food given to idols, we understand that an idol means nothing in the world, and there is only one God.
1 Corinthians 8:5 Even though some are known as gods, whether in heaven or on earth (and there are many gods and many lords),
1 Corinthians 8:6 For us, there is only one God, the Father, from whom everything comes, and we live for him; and there is one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom everything is made, and through whom we live.
1 Corinthians 8:7 However, not everyone knows this: some people still think of the meat as connected to the idol and eat it with that belief. Because their understanding is weak, it makes them feel guilty.
1 Corinthians 8:8 Eating food does not make us closer to God; if we eat, we are not better; if we don't eat, we are not worse.
1 Corinthians 8:9 But be careful that your freedom does not cause those who are weak to stumble.
1 Corinthians 8:10 If someone sees you with knowledge eating in the temple of an idol, won't a person with a weak conscience feel encouraged to eat food offered to idols?
1 Corinthians 8:11 Will your knowledge cause your weak brother to be lost, for whom Christ died?
1 Corinthians 8:12 But when you do wrong to your brothers and sisters, hurting their weak understanding, you also do wrong to Christ.
1 Corinthians 8:13 If food causes my brother to sin, I will not eat meat as long as the world exists, so I don't make my brother sin.
