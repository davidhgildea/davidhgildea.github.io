Ecclesiastes 2:1 I told myself, "Come on, let's try pleasure and have fun." But this, too, turned out to be meaningless.
Ecclesiastes 2:2 I said about laughter, "It's crazy," and about joy, "What does it do?"
Ecclesiastes 2:3 I decided to enjoy wine while keeping wise, and to embrace foolishness, until I could understand what is truly good for people to do throughout their lives.
Ecclesiastes 2:4 I made great projects; I built houses for myself; I planted vineyards for myself.
Ecclesiastes 2:5 I created gardens and orchards for myself, and in them I planted all types of fruit trees.
Ecclesiastes 2:6 I made pools of water to water the forest that grows trees.
Ecclesiastes 2:7 I got myself workers and maids, and had workers born in my house; I also had many herds of large and small animals, more than anyone in Jerusalem before me.
Ecclesiastes 2:8 I collected silver and gold for myself, and special treasures from kings and regions. I got male and female singers, and all kinds of musical pleasures that people enjoy.
Ecclesiastes 2:9 I became very great, more than anyone in Jerusalem before me; my wisdom stayed with me.
Ecclesiastes 2:10 Whatever my eyes wanted, I did not keep from them. I did not deny my heart any joy; my heart was happy in all my work, and this was my reward from all my work.
Ecclesiastes 2:11 I looked at everything I had done and all the hard work I had put in, and I saw that it was all pointless and frustrating, and there was no real gain to be had on earth.
Ecclesiastes 2:12 I looked to understand wisdom, madness, and folly: for what can a person do who follows the king? Only what has already been done.
Ecclesiastes 2:13 I saw that wisdom is better than foolishness, just as light is better than darkness.
Ecclesiastes 2:14 A wise person uses their mind, but a fool lives without guidance. I also noticed that the same things happen to everyone.
Ecclesiastes 2:15 I thought to myself, "What happens to fools will happen to me too. So why have I been so wise?" I realized that this is also meaningless.
Ecclesiastes 2:16 People won't remember the wise more than the fool forever; because what exists now will be forgotten in the future. And the wise person dies just like the fool.
Ecclesiastes 2:17 So I disliked life because the work done under the sun troubled me; it all seemed pointless and frustrating.
Ecclesiastes 2:18 Yes, I hated all my hard work under the sun because I must leave it to the person who will come after me.
Ecclesiastes 2:19 Who knows if he will be wise or foolish? Yet he will control all my hard work where I have been wise. This too is pointless.
Ecclesiastes 2:20 So, I lost hope over all the hard work I had done under the sun.
Ecclesiastes 2:21 There is a person who works with wisdom, knowledge, and fairness; but he will leave what he has worked for to another who hasn't worked for it. This too is meaningless and a big problem.
Ecclesiastes 2:22 What does a person gain from all their work and the troubles that make their heart worry, which they experience in their life under the sun?
Ecclesiastes 2:23 Every day is full of sadness and his work brings grief; even at night, his mind finds no rest. This too is meaningless.
Ecclesiastes 2:24 The best thing for a person is to eat, drink, and enjoy good things from their work. I saw that this too comes from God.
Ecclesiastes 2:25 Who can eat or enjoy life more than I?
Ecclesiastes 2:26 God gives a good person wisdom, knowledge, and happiness, but gives a sinner hard work to collect and pile up wealth only to hand it over to the one who pleases God. This too is meaningless and frustrating.
