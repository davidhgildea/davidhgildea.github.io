Ecclesiastes 1:1 This is the Preacher's message, David's son and king in Jerusalem.
Ecclesiastes 1:2 Everything is meaningless, says the Teacher; everything is completely meaningless.
Ecclesiastes 1:3 What good does a person get from all the work they do under the sun?
Ecclesiastes 1:4 One generation goes, and another comes, but the earth lasts forever.
Ecclesiastes 1:5 The sun rises and sets, and quickly goes back to where it rose.
Ecclesiastes 1:6 The wind moves south, then turns north; it goes around and around, and comes back to its paths.
Ecclesiastes 1:7 All rivers flow into the sea, but the sea never fills up. The rivers go back to where they came from and start again.
Ecclesiastes 1:8 Everything is hard work; people can't express it: the eye is never happy with what it sees, nor the ear with what it hears.
Ecclesiastes 1:9 What has happened before will happen again; what has been done before will be done again; there's nothing new under the sun.
Ecclesiastes 1:10 Can we say about anything, "Look, this is new"? It has already existed long ago, before our time.
Ecclesiastes 1:11 People do not remember past things; and those to come will not be remembered by those who follow them.
Ecclesiastes 1:12 I, the Teacher, was king over Israel in Jerusalem.
Ecclesiastes 1:13 I devoted myself to seek and understand by wisdom everything that happens on earth: this difficult task has been given by God to humanity to work on.
Ecclesiastes 1:14 I have seen everything people do on earth, and truly, everything is pointless and frustrating.
Ecclesiastes 1:15 What is twisted cannot be straightened; what is missing cannot be counted.
Ecclesiastes 1:16 I talked to myself, saying, "Look, I have become very wealthy and have gained more wisdom than anyone before me in Jerusalem; yes, my heart has learned much about wisdom and knowledge."
Ecclesiastes 1:17 I devoted myself to understand wisdom, madness, and foolishness and realized that this too is frustrating.
Ecclesiastes 1:18 With much wisdom comes much sadness; gaining more knowledge leads to more sorrow.
