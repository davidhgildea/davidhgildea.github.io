Hebrews 11:1 Faith is being sure of what we hope for and certain of what we do not see.
Hebrews 11:2 By it, the older people earned praise.
Hebrews 11:3 By believing, we know that God made the worlds with His command, so that what we see was not made from what we can see.
Hebrews 11:4 By trusting, Abel gave God a better offering than Cain, and it showed he was good, with God confirming his gifts; even after his death, he still speaks through it.
Hebrews 11:5 Through faith, Enoch was taken up so he wouldn't experience death; and he was not found, because God had taken him up. Before he was taken up, it was known that he pleased God.
Hebrews 11:6 Without faith, you cannot please God: whoever approaches God must believe that God exists and that he rewards those who earnestly search for him.
Hebrews 11:7 Trusting in God, Noah, who was warned about things still unseen, built an ark out of fear to save his family; by doing this, he showed the world was wrong and became the rightful owner of the goodness that comes from faith.
Hebrews 11:8 Abraham trusted God, and when he was told to go to a land he would later get as his own, he obeyed and left, even though he didn't know where he was going.
Hebrews 11:9 Through trust, he lived as a foreigner in the promised land, staying in tents with Isaac and Jacob, who were also promised the same inheritance.
Hebrews 11:10 He was looking for a city with solid foundations, built by God.
Hebrews 11:11 Sarah's faith gave her the power to have a child, even when she was too old, because she believed God who promised it.
Hebrews 11:12 From just one man, almost dead, came as many descendants as there are stars in the sky or grains of sand by the sea—too many to count.
Hebrews 11:13 All these people died still believing what God had promised them. They did not receive what was promised, but they saw it all from a distance and welcomed it. They agreed that they were just visitors and travelers on earth.
Hebrews 11:14 People who say these things make it clear that they are looking for a homeland.
Hebrews 11:15 Truly, if they had thought of the country they left, they could have found a chance to go back.
Hebrews 11:16 But now they want a better place, that is, a heavenly one. So, God is not ashamed to be called their God because he has made a city for them.
Hebrews 11:17 Abraham, because of his faith, when tested, was willing to sacrifice Isaac. And this man, who was given God's promises, was ready to sacrifice his only son.
Hebrews 11:18 God said your descendants will be named through Isaac.
Hebrews 11:19 Believing that God could bring him back to life, even from death; which is also how he figuratively got him back.
Hebrews 11:20 With trust, Isaac gave blessings to Jacob and Esau for their future.
Hebrews 11:21 In faith, when Jacob was near death, he gave a blessing to both of Joseph's sons and worshiped God, resting on his staff.
Hebrews 11:22 Joseph, trusting in God, talked about the Israelites leaving Egypt before he died, and gave instructions about what should be done with his bones.
Hebrews 11:23 Moses' parents hid him for three months after he was born because they saw he was a special child and they were not scared of the king's order.
Hebrews 11:24 By faith, when Moses grew up, he chose not to be called Pharaoh's daughter's son.
Hebrews 11:25 Choosing to endure hardship with God's people instead of enjoying short-lived sinful pleasures.
Hebrews 11:26 Valuing the shame of following Christ as greater wealth than the treasures of Egypt, because he looked forward to the reward.
Hebrews 11:27 Through trust, he left Egypt, not scared of the king's anger; because he stayed strong, as if seeing God who can't be seen.
Hebrews 11:28 By believing, he held the passover and put blood on the doorposts so the destroyer of the first children would not touch them.
Hebrews 11:29 Through faith, they crossed the Red Sea on dry ground, but when the Egyptians tried to do the same, they drowned.
Hebrews 11:30 By believing, the walls of Jericho fell after people marched around them for seven days.
Hebrews 11:31 By trusting, Rahab the prostitute did not die with those who did not believe, when she welcomed the spies peacefully.
Hebrews 11:32 What more can I say? I don't have enough time to talk about Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David, Samuel, and the prophets.
Hebrews 11:33 People with faith overcame kingdoms, did what is right, received what was promised, and shut lions' mouths.
Hebrews 11:34 Put out fires, avoided being killed by swords, became strong from being weak, grew brave in battle, and made foreign armies run away.
Hebrews 11:35 Women got back their dead, raised to life again. Others were tortured and did not accept rescue, so they could have a better resurrection.
Hebrews 11:36 Others faced mean teasing and whippings, and were also put in chains and jail.
Hebrews 11:37 They were hit with stones, cut into pieces, tested, and killed with swords. They moved around in sheep and goat skins, poor, suffering, and in pain.
Hebrews 11:38 The world did not deserve them: they roamed through deserts, in mountains, and in holes and caves of the ground.
Hebrews 11:39 Even though they were praised for their faith, they did not receive what God promised.
Hebrews 11:40 God has prepared something better for us, so that they would not reach perfection without us.
