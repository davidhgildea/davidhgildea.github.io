Romans 9:1 I speak the truth in Christ, I am not lying, my own conscience confirms it in the Holy Spirit.
Romans 9:2 I have great sadness and never-ending sorrow in my heart.
Romans 9:3 I could wish to be cut off from Christ for the sake of my brothers, my own family by blood.
Romans 9:4 They are Israelites, and to them belong the adoption as God's children, the glory, the agreements, the law, the worship of God, and the promises.
Romans 9:5 The fathers belong to them, and as a human, Christ came from them. He is above all, God forever praised. Amen.
Romans 9:6 It's not that God's message has failed. Not everyone from Israel truly belongs to Israel.
Romans 9:7 Just because they are Abraham's descendants doesn't mean they are all his true children. Instead, "Your true descendants will be through Isaac."
Romans 9:8 Those born from human desire are not God's children; only those born because of God's promise are considered true descendants.
Romans 9:9 This is the promised message: I will come at this time, and Sarah will have a son.
Romans 9:10 Not only that, but when Rebecca became pregnant by our father Isaac;
Romans 9:11 Before the children were born and had done anything good or bad, God's plan was based on his choice, not their actions, but on his call.
Romans 9:12 Someone told her, "The older will serve the younger."
Romans 9:13 It is written that I loved Jacob but did not love Esau.
Romans 9:14 What can we say? Is God unjust? No way.
Romans 9:15 God told Moses, "I will show mercy to those I choose, and I will show compassion to those I choose."
Romans 9:16 It is not about one's own desire or effort, but about God who gives mercy.
Romans 9:17 The Bible says to Pharaoh, I made you a leader for this reason: to show my power through you, so that my name would be known everywhere on earth.
Romans 9:18 So God shows mercy to whom he chooses, and he makes stubborn those he decides.
Romans 9:19 You might then ask me, why does he still blame us? Who has been able to resist his will?
Romans 9:20 No, but human, who are you to argue with God? Can the creation ask the creator, Why did you make me like this?
Romans 9:21 Doesn't the potter have the right to make from the same lump of clay one pot for special use and another for common use?
Romans 9:22 What if God wanted to show his anger and power but was very patient with those who deserved punishment?
Romans 9:23 God wanted to show the wealth of his glory to those he had chosen for mercy, whom he had prepared for glory.
Romans 9:24 Even us, whom God has called, not only from the Jews but also from the non-Jews?
Romans 9:25 In Osee, God also says, "I will name those 'my people' who were not my people; and those 'loved one' who were not loved."
Romans 9:26 It will happen that in the place where it was told to them, "You are not my people," there they will be called "children of the living God."
Romans 9:27 Isaiah also cries out about Israel, saying, even if the people of Israel are as many as the sand by the sea, only a small part will be saved.
Romans 9:28 God will complete the work and quickly do it with fairness; for God will carry out a brief task on the earth.
Romans 9:29 As Isaiah said earlier, if God had not left us some descendants, we would have become like Sodom and Gomorrah.
Romans 9:30 What can we say then? The non-Jews, who did not pursue being right with God, have achieved it through faith.
Romans 9:31 Israel, which chased the law of right living, did not reach the law of right living.
Romans 9:32 Why? Because they did not seek it with faith, but as if it could be earned by following the law. They tripped over that stone that makes people stumble.
Romans 9:33 It is written, "Look, I put in Zion a stone that makes people fall, and a rock that makes them trip: but anyone who trusts in him will not feel shame."
