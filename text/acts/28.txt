Acts 28:1 After they were safe, they learned that the island's name was Melita.
Acts 28:2 The local people were very kind to us. They made a fire and welcomed all of us because it was raining and cold.
Acts 28:3 When Paul collected some sticks and put them on the fire, a snake came out from the warmth and bit his hand.
Acts 28:4 When the local people saw the poisonous snake hanging from his hand, they said to each other, "Surely this man is a killer. Even though he survived the sea, justice will not let him live."
Acts 28:5 He threw the snake into the fire and was not hurt.
Acts 28:6 They expected him to swell up or suddenly drop dead, but when they watched for a long time and saw nothing bad happen to him, they changed their minds and said he was a god.
Acts 28:7 In that area, the island's leader, named Publius, owned land. He welcomed us and kindly let us stay for three days.
Acts 28:8 Publius's father was sick with a fever and severe diarrhea. Paul went to him, prayed, put his hands on him, and healed him.
Acts 28:9 After this happened, more people on the island who were sick came and were healed.
Acts 28:10 They also showed us much respect and gave us what we needed when we left.
Acts 28:11 After three months, we left on a ship from Alexandria that had spent the winter on the island, marked with the symbols Castor and Pollux.
Acts 28:12 We landed at Syracuse and stayed there for three days.
Acts 28:13 From there we circled around and reached Rhegium. After one day, a south wind started blowing, and the next day we arrived at Puteoli.
Acts 28:14 We met fellow believers who asked us to stay with them for seven days; then we continued on to Rome.
Acts 28:15 From there, when the believers heard about us, they came to meet us all the way to the Market of Appius and the Three Inns. When Paul saw them, he thanked God and felt encouraged.
Acts 28:16 When we arrived in Rome, the centurion handed the prisoners over to the guard's commander, but Paul was allowed to live alone with a soldier guarding him.
Acts 28:17 After three days, Paul gathered the Jewish leaders. Once they assembled, he told them, "Friends, although I did nothing against our people or our ancestors' ways, I was still handed over as a prisoner from Jerusalem to the Romans."
Acts 28:18 After they questioned me, they wanted to release me because they found no reason to kill me.
Acts 28:19 But when the Jews spoke against me, I had to ask for Caesar's judgment; not because I had any charges against my people.
Acts 28:20 I called for you so I could see and talk with you, because I am chained up due to the hope of Israel.
Acts 28:21 They told him, "We haven't gotten any letters from Judea about you, and none of our friends who have come here have spoken or said anything bad about you."
Acts 28:22 We want to hear what you think, because people everywhere speak against this group.
Acts 28:23 When they set a day, many came to his place. He explained the kingdom of God to them, convincing them about Jesus using the laws of Moses and the prophets, all day long.
Acts 28:24 Some believed what was said, and some did not believe.
Acts 28:25 When they could not agree, they left after Paul said, "The Holy Spirit was right to speak to our ancestors through the prophet Isaiah."
Acts 28:26 Go to these people and tell them, "Though you listen, you will not understand; though you look, you will not really see."
Acts 28:27 The hearts of these people have become hard, their ears are hard to hear, and they have shut their eyes. Otherwise, they might see with their eyes, hear with their ears, understand with their hearts, turn to me, and be healed by me.
Acts 28:28 Know this: God's salvation is sent to the non-Jews, and they will listen to it.
Acts 28:29 After he said this, the Jews left and had intense discussions with each other.
Acts 28:30 Paul lived for two full years in a house he rented, and he welcomed everyone who visited him.
Acts 28:31 Teaching about God's kingdom and explaining things about the Lord Jesus Christ with full confidence, and no one stopping him.
