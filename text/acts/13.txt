Acts 13:1 In the Antioch church, there were some prophets and teachers like Barnabas, Simeon also known as Niger, Lucius from Cyrene, Manaen who grew up with Herod the ruler, and Saul.
Acts 13:2 While they served God and went without food, the Holy Spirit said, "Set apart Barnabas and Saul for the job I have chosen them for."
Acts 13:3 After they fasted, prayed, and put their hands on them, they sent them off.
Acts 13:4 They were sent by the Holy Spirit and went to Seleucia; from there they sailed to Cyprus.
Acts 13:5 When they were in Salamis, they shared God's message in the Jewish synagogues and John helped them.
Acts 13:6 After they traveled across the island to Paphos, they met a magician who was a fake prophet and a Jew named Barjesus.
Acts 13:7 He was with the governor of the region, Sergius Paulus, a wise man. He asked for Barnabas and Saul because he wanted to hear God's message.
Acts 13:8 Elymas the magician tried to stop them and wanted to keep the governor from believing.
Acts 13:9 Then Saul, also known as Paul, filled with the Holy Spirit, looked straight at him.
Acts 13:10 And said, "Oh, you are very tricky and evil, you child of the devil and enemy of everything good, will you not stop twisting the true paths of God?"
Acts 13:11 Now, look, God's power is on you, and you will be blind and not see the sun for a while. Suddenly, he was covered in a fog and darkness, and he walked around looking for someone to guide him by the hand.
Acts 13:12 When the governor saw what happened, he believed and was amazed at God's teaching.
Acts 13:13 When Paul and his friends sailed from Paphos, they arrived at Perga in Pamphylia. John left them and went back to Jerusalem.
Acts 13:14 After leaving Perga, they arrived in Antioch in Pisidia, entered the synagogue on the Sabbath, and sat down.
Acts 13:15 After the reading of the law and the prophets, the synagogue leaders sent a message to them, saying, "Friends, if you have any encouraging message for the people, please speak."
Acts 13:16 Paul stood up, waved his hand, and said, "People of Israel and all who respect God, listen to me."
Acts 13:17 God chose our ancestors, honored the people while they lived as foreigners in Egypt, and with great power led them out of there.
Acts 13:18 For forty years God put up with their behavior in the desert.
Acts 13:19 After he defeated seven nations in Canaan, he gave their land to them by drawing lots.
Acts 13:20 After that, he gave them leaders for about 450 years, until Samuel the prophet.
Acts 13:21 Later they wanted a king, so God gave them Saul, son of Kish, from the tribe of Benjamin, for forty years.
Acts 13:22 After removing him, God made David their king and testified about him, saying, "I have found David, Jesse's son, a man who shares my desires and will do everything I want."
Acts 13:23 God promised to bring Israel a Savior, Jesus, from this man's descendants.
Acts 13:24 Before Jesus came, John told all the people of Israel to turn away from their sins and be baptized.
Acts 13:25 As John finished his work, he asked, "Who do you think I am? I am not the one. But look, someone is coming after me, and I'm not good enough to untie his sandals."
Acts 13:26 Friends and descendants of Abraham, and all who honor God, this message of salvation is for you.
Acts 13:27 The people living in Jerusalem and their leaders didn't recognize him or understand what the prophets said, even though they read their words every Sabbath. By condemning him, they made these prophecies come true.
Acts 13:28 Even though they could not prove he deserved to die, they still asked Pilate to have him killed.
Acts 13:29 After completing all that was written about him, they removed him from the cross and placed him in a tomb.
Acts 13:30 God raised him from the dead.
Acts 13:31 He was seen for many days by those who went with him from Galilee to Jerusalem, who now tell the people about him.
Acts 13:32 We share with you good news about the promise given to our ancestors.
Acts 13:33 God has done what he promised our ancestors by raising Jesus from the dead, as it's written in the second psalm: "You are my Son; today I have become your father."
Acts 13:34 About raising him from the dead, never to decay again, he said, I will give you the faithful blessings promised to David.
Acts 13:35 That's why it is said in another psalm, "You will not let your Holy One decay."
Acts 13:36 David served his time by God's will, then he died, was buried with his ancestors, and his body decayed.
Acts 13:37 But he, who was raised by God, did not decay.
Acts 13:38 Let it be clear to you then, friends, that through this man the forgiveness of sins is announced to you.
Acts 13:39 By him, all who believe are made right with God from everything that the law of Moses could not make right.
Acts 13:40 Be careful, so what the prophets said doesn't happen to you.
Acts 13:41 Look, you who scoff, be amazed and vanish: because I am doing something in your days, something you will never believe, even if someone explains it to you.
Acts 13:42 After the Jews left the synagogue, the non-Jews asked to hear these teachings the following Sabbath.
Acts 13:43 After the meeting ended, many Jews and believers followed Paul and Barnabas. They talked to them and encouraged them to keep living in God's kindness.
Acts 13:44 On the next Sabbath, nearly the whole city gathered to hear God's message.
Acts 13:45 When the Jews saw the crowds, they felt jealous and spoke against what Paul was saying, arguing and insulting God.
Acts 13:46 Paul and Barnabas became brave and said, "It was important to speak God's word to you first. But since you reject it and think you're not worthy of eternal life, now we will speak to other people."
Acts 13:47 God has instructed us, saying, I have made you a light for the non-Jews, so that you may bring salvation to the farthest places of the world.
Acts 13:48 When the non-Jews heard this, they were happy and praised God's message. All those chosen for eternal life believed.
Acts 13:49 God's message spread all over the area.
Acts 13:50 The Jews convinced the religious and respected women and the city's leaders to persecute Paul and Barnabas and forced them to leave their region.
Acts 13:51 They brushed the dust from their feet as a warning to them and went to Iconium.
Acts 13:52 The followers were filled with happiness and the Holy Spirit.
