Genesis 26:1 There was not enough food in the country, in addition to the earlier food shortage during Abraham's time. Isaac went to see Abimelech, the king of the Philistines, in Gerar.
Genesis 26:2 God appeared to him and said, "Do not go down to Egypt; live in the land I will tell you about."
Genesis 26:3 Stay in this land, and I will be with you and bless you. I will give all these countries to you and your descendants, and I will keep the promise I made to your father Abraham.
Genesis 26:4 I will make your children as many as the stars in the sky, and I will give them all these lands; through your descendants, all the world's nations will be blessed.
Genesis 26:5 Abraham listened to me and followed my orders, commands, rules, and laws.
Genesis 26:6 Isaac lived in Gerar.
Genesis 26:7 The local people asked him about his wife; he said, "She is my sister," because he was afraid to say, "She is my wife." He thought they might kill him because of Rebekah, as she was very beautiful.
Genesis 26:8 When he had been there a long time, King Abimelech of the Philistines looked out a window and saw Isaac being affectionate with his wife Rebekah.
Genesis 26:9 Abimelech called Isaac and said, "She is really your wife; why did you say, 'She is my sister'?" Isaac answered, "I thought I might be killed because of her."
Genesis 26:10 Abimelech asked, "What have you done to us? Someone could easily have slept with your wife, and you would have made us guilty."
Genesis 26:11 Abimelech told everyone, "Anyone who harms this man or his wife will definitely be killed."
Genesis 26:12 Then Isaac planted crops in that land, and in that same year, he got a hundred times more than he planted, and God blessed him.
Genesis 26:13 The man became very successful and continued to prosper until he was extremely wealthy.
Genesis 26:14 He owned many sheep, cattle, and had many workers: the Philistines were jealous of him.
Genesis 26:15 All the wells that his father's servants had dug during Abraham's time, the Philistines had blocked them and filled them with dirt.
Genesis 26:16 Abimelech said to Isaac, "Leave us; you are much stronger than we are."
Genesis 26:17 Isaac left that place and set up his tent in the Gerar valley and lived there.
Genesis 26:18 Isaac reopened the water wells that were dug in his father Abraham's time, because the Philistines had filled them after Abraham died. He named them what his father had named them.
Genesis 26:19 Isaac's workers dug in the valley and found a well with flowing water.
Genesis 26:20 The herders from Gerar fought with Isaac's herders, saying, "The water is ours!" So, he named the well Esek because they argued with him.
Genesis 26:21 They dug another well, and people also fought over it; so he named it Sitnah.
Genesis 26:22 He moved from there, dug another well, and no one fought over it. He named it Rehoboth, saying, "Now God has given us space, and we will grow in this land."
Genesis 26:23 He left that place and went to Beersheba.
Genesis 26:24 That night, God appeared to him and said, "I am the God of your father Abraham. Do not be afraid, for I am with you. I will bless you and give you many descendants to honor my servant Abraham."
Genesis 26:25 He built an altar there, called on God's name, set up his tent, and Isaac's servants dug a well.
Genesis 26:26 Abimelech from Gerar, his friend Ahuzzath, and Phichol, his army's leader, went to him.
Genesis 26:27 Isaac asked them, "Why do you come to me if you hate me and have made me leave you?"
Genesis 26:28 They said, "We clearly saw that God was with you, so we thought, let's make a promise between us and you, and let's agree together."
Genesis 26:29 Please do us no harm, as we have not harmed you and have only treated you well, sending you off peacefully. You are now blessed by God.
Genesis 26:30 He prepared a meal for them, and they ate and drank.
Genesis 26:31 They got up early in the morning, made promises to each other, and then Isaac sent them away, and they left him peacefully.
Genesis 26:32 That same day, Isaac's workers came and told him about the well they had dug, and said to him, "We have found water."
Genesis 26:33 He named it Shebah, so the city is called Beersheba to this day.
Genesis 26:34 Esau was forty when he married Judith, Beeri the Hittite's daughter, and Bashemath, Elon the Hittite's daughter.
Genesis 26:35 They made Isaac and Rebekah very unhappy.
