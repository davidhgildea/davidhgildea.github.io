Genesis 37:1 Jacob lived in the land where his father had been a foreigner, in Canaan.
Genesis 37:2 This lists Jacob's family. Seventeen-year-old Joseph was tending sheep with his brothers, the sons of Bilhah and Zilpah, his father's wives. He told his father about the bad things they were doing.
Genesis 37:3 Israel loved Joseph more than his other children because Joseph was born when Israel was already old, and he gave Joseph a colorful coat.
Genesis 37:4 When his brothers saw that their father loved him more than them, they hated him and could not speak kindly to him.
Genesis 37:5 Joseph had a dream and shared it with his brothers, which made them hate him even more.
Genesis 37:6 He said to them, "Please listen to this dream I had."
Genesis 37:7 Look, we were tying up bundles of grain in the field when suddenly my bundle stood up straight. Then your bundles gathered around and bowed to mine.
Genesis 37:8 His brothers asked him, "Will you really rule over us? Will you actually be our boss?" They disliked him even more because of his dreams and what he said.
Genesis 37:9 He had another dream and shared it with his brothers, saying, "Look, I dreamed again; the sun, the moon, and eleven stars bowed down to me."
Genesis 37:10 He told his father and brothers, and his father scolded him, saying, "What kind of dream did you have? Will your mother, brothers and I actually come to bow down to you on the ground?"
Genesis 37:11 His brothers were jealous of him, but his father paid attention to what was said.
Genesis 37:12 His brothers went to graze their father's sheep in Shechem.
Genesis 37:13 Israel said to Joseph, "Aren't your brothers pasturing the sheep in Shechem? Come, I'll send you to them." And he replied, "I'm here."
Genesis 37:14 He told him, "Please go and check if your brothers and the flocks are okay; then come back and tell me." So he sent him from the Hebron valley, and he went to Shechem.
Genesis 37:15 A man found him lost in the field and asked, "What are you looking for?"
Genesis 37:16 He said, "I'm looking for my brothers. Please tell me where they are taking care of their sheep."
Genesis 37:17 The man said, "They left this place; I heard them plan to go to Dothan." So Joseph followed his brothers and found them in Dothan.
Genesis 37:18 When they saw him from far away, before he got close to them, they planned together to kill him.
Genesis 37:19 They said to each other, "Look, here comes the dreamer."
Genesis 37:20 Let's go now, kill him, throw him into a pit, and say a wild animal ate him. Then we'll see what happens to his dreams.
Genesis 37:21 Reuben heard them and saved him from their hands, saying, Let's not kill him.
Genesis 37:22 Reuben told them, "Don't kill him. Just throw him into this pit in the desert, but don't hurt him." He planned to save him from them and return him to his father.
Genesis 37:23 When Joseph reached his brothers, they took off his colorful coat that he was wearing.
Genesis 37:24 They grabbed him and threw him into a hole. The hole was empty without any water in it.
Genesis 37:25 They sat down to eat bread, and when they looked up, they saw a group of Ishmaelites coming from Gilead with their camels carrying spices, balm, and myrrh to take to Egypt.
Genesis 37:26 Judah asked his brothers, "What do we gain if we kill our brother and hide his blood?"
Genesis 37:27 Come, let's sell him to the Ishmaelites and not hurt him; he is our brother, our own flesh. And his brothers agreed.
Genesis 37:28 Some Midianite traders passed by; they pulled Joseph out of the hole and sold him to the Ishmaelites for twenty silver coins, and they took Joseph to Egypt.
Genesis 37:29 Reuben went back to the pit and saw that Joseph was not there, so he tore his clothes.
Genesis 37:30 He went back to his brothers and said, "The boy is gone; where can I go now?"
Genesis 37:31 They took Joseph's coat, killed a young goat, and dipped the coat in the blood.
Genesis 37:32 They sent the colorful coat and took it to their father, saying, "We found this. See if it's your son's coat or not."
Genesis 37:33 He recognized it and said, "This is my son's coat; a wild animal must have eaten him. Joseph has surely been torn apart."
Genesis 37:34 Jacob tore his clothes, wore rough fabric around his waist, and grieved for his son for many days.
Genesis 37:35 All his sons and daughters tried to comfort him, but he wouldn't be comforted and said, "I will mourn for my son until I die." So he cried for his son.
Genesis 37:36 The Midianites sold him to Potiphar in Egypt, who was an official of the king and the captain of the guard.
