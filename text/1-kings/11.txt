1 Kings 11:1 King Solomon loved many foreign women, including Pharaoh's daughter and women from Moab, Ammon, Edom, Sidon, and Hittite lands.
1 Kings 11:2 About the countries which God told the people of Israel, "Do not associate with them, nor let them associate with you, because they will surely turn your hearts towards their gods," Solomon became very attached to them because of love.
1 Kings 11:3 He had seven hundred wives of royal birth and three hundred secondary wives, and his wives led his heart astray.
1 Kings 11:4 When Solomon was old, his wives made his heart follow other gods, and his heart was not as faithful to God as his father David's heart was.
1 Kings 11:5 Solomon followed Ashtoreth, the Zidonians' goddess, and Milcom, the Ammonites' hated idol.
1 Kings 11:6 Solomon did bad things that God saw, and he didn't follow God completely like his father David did.
1 Kings 11:7 Solomon built a worship site on the hill facing Jerusalem for Chemosh, the Moabites' detested god, and for Molech, the Ammonites' detested god.
1 Kings 11:8 He did the same for all his foreign wives, who burned incense and offered sacrifices to their gods.
1 Kings 11:9 God was angry with Solomon because Solomon's heart turned away from the God of Israel, who had appeared to him twice.
1 Kings 11:10 God told him not to follow other gods, but he did not obey what God commanded.
1 Kings 11:11 So God said to Solomon, "Because you have done this and have not followed my agreement and my rules that I gave you, I will certainly take the kingdom away from you and give it to your servant."
1 Kings 11:12 Despite this, I will not do it during your lifetime out of respect for your father David; instead, I will take it away from your son.
1 Kings 11:13 However, I will not take away the entire kingdom; I will give one tribe to your son because of my servant David, and for Jerusalem, which I have chosen.
1 Kings 11:14 God made Hadad the Edomite, of royal blood from Edom, become Solomon's enemy.
1 Kings 11:15 When David was in Edom and Joab, the army commander, had gone to bury the dead after defeating every man in Edom.
1 Kings 11:16 Joab stayed there with all Israel for six months, until he had killed every man in Edom.
1 Kings 11:17 Hadad ran away to Egypt with some of his father's Edomite servants while he was still a young child.
1 Kings 11:18 They left Midian and went to Paran, taking people with them from Paran to Egypt, to Pharaoh, the king of Egypt. Pharaoh gave him a house, food, and land.
1 Kings 11:19 Hadad was liked very much by Pharaoh, so he let him marry his wife's sister, Queen Tahpenes's sister.
1 Kings 11:20 Tahpenes' sister had a son named Genubath for him, and Tahpenes raised Genubath in the Pharaoh's palace; Genubath grew up among the Pharaoh's sons.
1 Kings 11:21 When Hadad in Egypt learned that David had died and that Joab, the army commander, was also dead, he said to Pharaoh, "Let me go back to my own country."
1 Kings 11:22 Then Pharaoh asked him, "What have you missed with me, that you want to go back to your own country?" He replied, "Nothing, but please let me go."
1 Kings 11:23 God raised up another enemy against him, Rezon son of Eliadah, who had run away from his master Hadadezer, the king of Zobah.
1 Kings 11:24 He brought men together, became their leader, and ruled in Damascus after David defeated the people of Zobah. They lived there in Damascus.
1 Kings 11:25 He opposed Israel throughout Solomon's reign, causing trouble like Hadad, hated Israel, and ruled over Syria.
1 Kings 11:26 Jeroboam, Nebat's son from Zereda in Ephraim, who worked for Solomon and whose mother Zeruah was a widow, rebelled against the king.
1 Kings 11:27 He turned against the king because: Solomon had built Millo and fixed the damaged parts of his father David's city.
1 Kings 11:28 Jeroboam was a brave and strong man. Solomon noticed that he was a hard worker, so he put him in charge of all the work for Joseph's descendants.
1 Kings 11:29 At that time, when Jeroboam left Jerusalem, the prophet Ahijah from Shiloh found him on the road; Ahijah was wearing a new cloak, and they were alone in the field.
1 Kings 11:30 Ahijah took the new clothing he was wearing and tore it into twelve pieces.
1 Kings 11:31 He told Jeroboam, "Take ten pieces: God, the God of Israel, says, 'Look, I will take the kingdom from Solomon's hand and give you ten tribes.'"
1 Kings 11:32 He will keep one tribe for my servant David and for Jerusalem, the city I chose from all the tribes of Israel.
1 Kings 11:33 Because they left me and worshiped Ashtoreth the goddess of the Sidonians, Chemosh the god of the Moabites, and Milcom the god of the Ammonites, and did not follow my ways, doing what is right in my sight, and keeping my laws and rules, like David their ancestor did.
1 Kings 11:34 However, I won't take the entire kingdom from him. Instead, I will let him be a leader all his life because of my servant David, whom I selected, since he followed my commands and rules.
1 Kings 11:35 I will take the kingdom from his son's hand and will give it to you, ten tribes.
1 Kings 11:36 I will give one tribe to his son so that my servant David may always have a presence in Jerusalem, the city where I chose to honor my name.
1 Kings 11:37 I will take you, and you will rule as you wish, and be king over Israel.
1 Kings 11:38 If you listen to all I tell you, follow my path, do what is right in my eyes, and keep my laws and commands like my servant David did; then I will be with you, build you a strong home, just like I did for David, and give you Israel.
1 Kings 11:39 I will punish David's descendants for a while, but not forever.
1 Kings 11:40 Solomon wanted to kill Jeroboam. So Jeroboam got up, ran away to Egypt, to King Shishak, and stayed there until Solomon died.
1 Kings 11:41 The other things Solomon did, all his actions, and his wisdom, are they not recorded in the book about Solomon's deeds?
1 Kings 11:42 Solomon ruled in Jerusalem over all Israel for forty years.
1 Kings 11:43 Solomon died and was buried in his father David's city, and his son Rehoboam became king after him.
