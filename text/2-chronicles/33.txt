2 Chronicles 33:1 Manasseh was twelve years old when he became king, and he ruled for fifty-five years in Jerusalem.
2 Chronicles 33:2 But did what was bad in God's eyes, just like the horrible things the non-Israelites did, whom God had driven out before the Israelites.
2 Chronicles 33:3 He rebuilt the worship places his father Hezekiah had destroyed, set up altars for Baal, made sacred gardens, and bowed to the stars of the sky, serving them.
2 Chronicles 33:4 He also made places to worship in God's house, where God had said, "My name will always be in Jerusalem."
2 Chronicles 33:5 He made altars for all the heavenly bodies in the two yards of God's house.
2 Chronicles 33:6 He made his children go through fire in Hinnom's valley, practiced fortune-telling, used magic, did witchcraft, and talked to spirits and sorcerers. He did a lot of bad things that made God very angry.
2 Chronicles 33:7 He placed a carved idol, which he made, in God's house, where God told David and Solomon, "In this house and in Jerusalem, which I chose from all Israel's tribes, my name will remain forever."
2 Chronicles 33:8 I will not make Israel leave the land I gave to your ancestors again, if they follow all my commands, laws, and rules given through Moses.
2 Chronicles 33:9 Manasseh led Judah and the people of Jerusalem to make bigger mistakes than the nations God had removed before the Israelites.
2 Chronicles 33:10 God spoke to Manasseh and his people, but they did not listen.
2 Chronicles 33:11 So God let the commanders of the Assyrian king's army come, who captured Manasseh in the bushes, tied him up with chains, and took him to Babylon.
2 Chronicles 33:12 When he suffered, he asked God for help and humbled himself a lot before the God of his ancestors.
2 Chronicles 33:13 He prayed to God, and God listened to him, heard his plea, and brought him back to his own kingdom in Jerusalem. Then Manasseh understood that God was the true God.
2 Chronicles 33:14 After this, he built a wall outside the city of David, west of Gihon in the valley, up to the Fish Gate entrance, around Ophel, making it very tall, and placed military leaders in all of Judah's fortified cities.
2 Chronicles 33:15 He removed the foreign gods and the idol from God's house, and all the altars that he had built on the hill of God's house, and in Jerusalem, and threw them out of the city.
2 Chronicles 33:16 He fixed God's altar, offered peace and thank you sacrifices on it, and told Judah to worship the God of Israel.
2 Chronicles 33:17 People still made sacrifices in the high places, but only to God.
2 Chronicles 33:18 The other things Manasseh did, his prayer to God, and what the prophets told him in God's name are written in the book of the kings of Israel.
2 Chronicles 33:19 His prayer, how God listened to him, all his sins, his wrongdoing, and the spots where he made worship sites, and put up wooded areas and carved idols, before he became humble: look, they are recorded in the prophets' writings.
2 Chronicles 33:20 Manasseh died and was buried in his own home; then his son Amon became king after him.
2 Chronicles 33:21 Amon was 22 years old when he became king, and he ruled for two years in Jerusalem.
2 Chronicles 33:22 But he did evil things that God didn't like, just like his father Manasseh: Amon worshiped all the idols his father Manasseh had made, and followed them.
2 Chronicles 33:23 He did not humble himself before God, unlike his father Manasseh; instead, Amon kept sinning even more.
2 Chronicles 33:24 His servants plotted against him and killed him in his house.
2 Chronicles 33:25 The people of the land killed all who plotted against King Amon, and they made his son Josiah the new king.
