<p class="verse" id="v1"><a class="verse-num" href="#v1" aria-label="Leviticus 2:1">1</a> When someone wants to give a grain offering to God, it must be of fine flour; they are to pour oil on it and add frankincense.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v2"><a class="verse-num" href="#v2" aria-label="Leviticus 2:2">2</a> He must take it to the priests, Aaron&#39;s sons. He must scoop out a handful of the flour and oil, along with all the frankincense. The priest will then burn this portion on the altar as a fire offering, a pleasing aroma to God.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v3"><a class="verse-num" href="#v3" aria-label="Leviticus 2:3">3</a> Aaron and his sons will get the rest of the grain offering; it is a very holy part of the offerings to God made with fire.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v4"><a class="verse-num" href="#v4" aria-label="Leviticus 2:4">4</a> If you offer a grain gift cooked in an oven, it should be flatbreads without yeast made from fine flour mixed with oil, or thin crackers without yeast spread with oil.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v5"><a class="verse-num" href="#v5" aria-label="Leviticus 2:5">5</a> If your offering is a grain baked in a pan, it should be made of pure flour without yeast, mixed with oil.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v6"><a class="verse-num" href="#v6" aria-label="Leviticus 2:6">6</a> You will cut it into pieces and pour oil on it: it is a food offering.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v7"><a class="verse-num" href="#v7" aria-label="Leviticus 2:7">7</a> If your gift is a grain offering cooked in a pan, make it with fine flour and oil.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v8"><a class="verse-num" href="#v8" aria-label="Leviticus 2:8">8</a> Bring the grain offering made from these things to God. When you give it to the priest, he will take it to the altar.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v9"><a class="verse-num" href="#v9" aria-label="Leviticus 2:9">9</a> The priest will take a part of the grain offering to remember it, and burn it on the altar. It is a fire offering, a pleasing aroma to God.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v10"><a class="verse-num" href="#v10" aria-label="Leviticus 2:10">10</a> Aaron and his sons can have what&#39;s left of the grain offering; it is very holy and comes from the offerings made to God by fire.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v11"><a class="verse-num" href="#v11" aria-label="Leviticus 2:11">11</a> Do not use yeast when you make any food offering for God. You must not burn any yeast or honey in a fire offering to God.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v12"><a class="verse-num" href="#v12" aria-label="Leviticus 2:12">12</a> For the offering of the first crops, give them to God, but do not burn them on the altar as a pleasing aroma.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v13"><a class="verse-num" href="#v13" aria-label="Leviticus 2:13">13</a> In all grain offerings you make, you must use salt. Do not forget to add the salt that represents your agreement with God. Include salt with all your offerings.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v14"><a class="verse-num" href="#v14" aria-label="Leviticus 2:14">14</a> If you bring a grain offering of your first crops to God, you must present roasted green heads of grain as the grain offering from your first crops.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v15"><a class="verse-num" href="#v15" aria-label="Leviticus 2:15">15</a> Put oil on it and place frankincense on top: it is a food offering.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v16"><a class="verse-num" href="#v16" aria-label="Leviticus 2:16">16</a> The priest will burn some of the crushed grain and some of the oil, along with all the incense, as a reminder. This is a fire offering to God.</p>
