<p class="verse" id="v1"><a class="verse-num" href="#v1" aria-label="Isaiah 20:1">1</a> In the year Tartan came to Ashdod, sent by Sargon, the king of Assyria, and attacked and captured it.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v2"><a class="verse-num" href="#v2" aria-label="Isaiah 20:2">2</a> At that time, God told Isaiah, son of Amoz, &quot;Go, take the sackcloth off your waist, and remove your shoe from your foot.&quot; And he did it, going around naked and without shoes.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v3"><a class="verse-num" href="#v3" aria-label="Isaiah 20:3">3</a> And God said, &quot;Just as my servant Isaiah walked without clothes and barefoot for three years as a sign and miracle for Egypt and Ethiopia;</p>

<p class="verse" id="v4"><a class="verse-num" href="#v4" aria-label="Isaiah 20:4">4</a> The king of Assyria will take the Egyptians and Ethiopians as prisoners, both young and old, without clothes and shoes, their backsides exposed, to embarrass Egypt.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v5"><a class="verse-num" href="#v5" aria-label="Isaiah 20:5">5</a> They will feel fear and shame about their hope in Ethiopia and their pride in Egypt.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v6"><a class="verse-num" href="#v6" aria-label="Isaiah 20:6">6</a> On that day, the people of this island will say, &quot;Look, this is what we hoped for, where we ran for help to save us from the king of Assyria: but how can we escape?&quot;</p>
