<p class="verse" id="v1"><a class="verse-num" href="#v1" aria-label="Isaiah 23:1">1</a> The weight of Tyre’s troubles. Cry out, ships of Tarshish; for it is destroyed, with no homes or way to enter: from the land of Chittim, they learn of it.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v2"><a class="verse-num" href="#v2" aria-label="Isaiah 23:2">2</a> Stay calm, people of the island; you whom the sea-traveling merchants of Sidon have filled up.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v3"><a class="verse-num" href="#v3" aria-label="Isaiah 23:3">3</a> By large waters, the crops of the Nile, the river&#39;s yield, bring her income; she is a marketplace for nations.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v4"><a class="verse-num" href="#v4" aria-label="Isaiah 23:4">4</a> Feel embarrassed, O Sidon, because the sea has claimed, even its mighty waves, saying, I do not struggle in labor, nor give birth to children, and I do not raise young men or nurture young women.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v5"><a class="verse-num" href="#v5" aria-label="Isaiah 23:5">5</a> Just like the news about Egypt hurt them, they will be very upset by the news about Tyre.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v6"><a class="verse-num" href="#v6" aria-label="Isaiah 23:6">6</a> Go to Tarshish; cry out, you people of the island.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v7"><a class="verse-num" href="#v7" aria-label="Isaiah 23:7">7</a> Is this your happy city, which is very old? It will walk far away to stay.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v8"><a class="verse-num" href="#v8" aria-label="Isaiah 23:8">8</a> Who has planned this against Tyre, the city known for crowns, where the merchants are like leaders, and the traders respected throughout the world?</p>

<p class="verse" id="v9"><a class="verse-num" href="#v9" aria-label="Isaiah 23:9">9</a> God has planned it, to tarnish all pride and to disgrace all respected people on earth.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v10"><a class="verse-num" href="#v10" aria-label="Isaiah 23:10">10</a> Go through your land like a river, O daughter of Tarshish: you have no more power.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v11"><a class="verse-num" href="#v11" aria-label="Isaiah 23:11">11</a> He reached out his hand over the sea and disturbed nations: God has ordered to destroy the merchant city and its strong defenses.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v12"><a class="verse-num" href="#v12" aria-label="Isaiah 23:12">12</a> He said, &quot;You won&#39;t be happy anymore, you mistreated young woman of Sidon. Get up, go over to Cyprus; even there you won&#39;t find peace.&quot;</p>

<p class="verse" id="v13"><a class="verse-num" href="#v13" aria-label="Isaiah 23:13">13</a> Look at the land of the Chaldeans; these people did not exist until the Assyrian built it for the desert dwellers: they built its towers, they built its palaces; and it was destroyed.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v14"><a class="verse-num" href="#v14" aria-label="Isaiah 23:14">14</a> Cry out, you ships of Tarshish: because your power is destroyed.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v15"><a class="verse-num" href="#v15" aria-label="Isaiah 23:15">15</a> On that day, Tyre will be forgotten for seventy years, the length of one king&#39;s reign. After seventy years are over, Tyre will return to life like a prostitute.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v16"><a class="verse-num" href="#v16" aria-label="Isaiah 23:16">16</a> Play the harp, walk around the town, forgotten woman; play lovely tunes, sing a lot of songs, so you will be remembered.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v17"><a class="verse-num" href="#v17" aria-label="Isaiah 23:17">17</a> After seventy years, God will return to Tyre, and it will start earning money again by interacting with all the world&#39;s kingdoms on the earth.</p>

<p class="verse" id="v18"><a class="verse-num" href="#v18" aria-label="Isaiah 23:18">18</a> Her goods and earnings will be sacred to God; they will not be stored or hoarded. Her goods will provide enough food and lasting clothes for those living in God’s presence.</p>
